# Delivery Model Comparison

## Case A — Government document management system under a tender

**Selected model: Predictive**

| Criterion | Justification |
|---|---|
| Requirements uncertainty | Low to medium. Tender documentation normally defines the required scope and acceptance criteria in advance. |
| Cost of error | High. Errors can lead to failed acceptance, delays and contract problems. |
| Regulation | High. Government information systems may have formal security, documentation and procurement requirements. |
| Customer maturity | Formal governance and approval procedures are expected. |
| Team distribution | Several parties may participate: customer, contractor, security and infrastructure teams. |
| Contract type | Usually fixed scope and formal acceptance under a tender. |

**Conclusion:** Predictive delivery is appropriate because requirements, milestones and acceptance procedures need to be controlled in advance.

## Case B — New mobile startup product with an unverified hypothesis

**Selected model: Adaptive**

| Criterion | Justification |
|---|---|
| Requirements uncertainty | Very high. The product hypothesis has not yet been validated. |
| Cost of error | Medium. The main risk is spending time and money on a product users do not need. |
| Regulation | Usually low compared with banking or government projects. |
| Customer maturity | Product decisions may change quickly based on user feedback. |
| Team distribution | A small cross-functional startup team can work iteratively. |
| Contract type | Scope is usually flexible and focused on product learning rather than fixed delivery. |

**Conclusion:** Adaptive delivery is appropriate because the team needs short iterations, experiments and frequent user feedback.

## Case C — Migration of a bank monolith to the cloud

**Selected model: Hybrid**

| Criterion | Justification |
|---|---|
| Requirements uncertainty | Medium. The target is known, but technical migration details may change during implementation. |
| Cost of error | Very high. Failure can affect critical banking services and data. |
| Regulation | High. Security, availability, audit and data requirements must be controlled. |
| Customer maturity | Multiple technical and business stakeholders are involved. |
| Team distribution | Infrastructure, application, security, operations and vendor teams may work together. |
| Contract type | Major milestones can be fixed while technical implementation remains iterative. |

**Conclusion:** Hybrid delivery is appropriate because governance and major migration milestones need predictability, while technical migration work benefits from iterative execution.
